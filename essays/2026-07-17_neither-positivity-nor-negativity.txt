positivity is not always good, and negativity is not always evil.

actually, none really matters for achieving objectives in general. what matters is data.

if you are feeling positive, it does not always mean you are going in the right direction, while feeling negative does not mean you are wrong. you might be in utmost pain but progressing (workouts), while at the same time being wrong (suppressing signals of a serious illness with long-term effects).

feelings, in general, are a bad sample to measure reality.

feelings != truth

a better way is actually much simpler than biochemical fluctuations inside the body, which is measurements. the more focused, the better. each iteration should yield some data and check the delta between your assumptions and reality. the narrower the gap, the better.

simply: make an assumption, test the theory, measure the gap, learn, try again with a better way, gradually close the gap.

simpler: raw assumption -> focused test -> detailed measurement -> learning -> repeat
